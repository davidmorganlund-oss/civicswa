About CivicsWA

A free, independent guide to when Washington’s public bodies meet and how to take part.

Who runs it

CivicsWA is a service of BarNone, Inc., an independent nonprofit. It is not a government website. It is not affiliated with any government agency, and it does not speak for any public office. It doesn’t take sides on candidates, parties, bills or ballot measures.

Where the meetings come from

Every listing comes from a public body’s own published calendar: city and county meeting portals, school district calendars, library board schedules and the Legislature’s committee schedule. Each night, CivicsWA reads those calendars and updates the site. Every meeting links back to the host’s own page.

A few public bodies publish a standing schedule (“the second and fourth Tuesday at 10 a.m.”) instead of a calendar. Those meetings are marked Regular schedule: confirm first, because they can be cancelled or moved without the change reaching us.

When a meeting falls on a state holiday, state law moves it to the next business day, and we show it there.

How current it is

Listings refresh every night. If a calendar can’t be reached, we keep showing what it listed last time and keep trying. The coverage page shows every calendar we read and when each was last read successfully.

Always confirm with the host before you go. Agendas, times and rooms change, sometimes the same day.

What’s not here yet

Some public bodies only post PDFs, or don’t post a calendar online at all. Some websites block automated readers, and we respect that. Some school districts publish only on systems that ask automated readers to stay out. For those places, the site links to their own meeting pages instead. The coverage page lists what’s included county by county.

Found a mistake or a missing meeting?

Report a correction

Email admin@barnoneinc.org. Tell us what’s wrong and where, and include a link to the right information if you have one.

Privacy

CivicsWA has no accounts and no ads, and it doesn’t sell or share information about visitors. The county and city you pick are saved only in your own browser, so the site remembers them next time. You can clear them by clearing your browser’s site data. When you look up your address, it goes from your browser to the U.S. Census Bureau’s address lookup, and only the map point it returns goes to the Washington Department of Revenue and WSDOT maps to find special districts. CivicsWA never receives or keeps your address. If we add visitor counts, we will use a service that doesn’t use cookies or track you across other websites, and this page will say so.

Accessibility

We aim to meet the Web Content Accessibility Guidelines (WCAG) 2.1, level AA. Pages work with a keyboard and screen readers, text meets contrast standards in light and dark mode, and the site works on phones. If something gets in your way, please tell us and we’ll fix it.

Guides are written in plain language, list their sources, and are built to work with screen readers.
