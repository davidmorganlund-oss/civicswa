Which state agency does what

Washington has dozens of state agencies. Here is a short guide to the ones people most often need, and where to go when you're stuck.

Common needs

  • Driver's licenses, IDs and vehicle registration: Department of Licensing.
  • Food, cash and child care assistance: Department of Social and Health Services (DSHS).
  • Apple Health (Medicaid): Health Care Authority.
  • Unemployment and paid family and medical leave: Employment Security Department.
  • Workplace safety, wages and workers' compensation: Department of Labor and Industries.
  • Public health, health professional licenses, birth and death records: Department of Health.
  • Taxes and business licenses: Department of Revenue.
  • K-12 schools: Office of Superintendent of Public Instruction (OSPI).
  • Prisons and community supervision: Department of Corrections.
  • Highways and ferries: Department of Transportation (WSDOT).
  • Air, water and cleanup: Department of Ecology.
  • Housing and community grants: Department of Commerce.
  • Insurance problems: Office of the Insurance Commissioner.
  • Consumer protection and civil rights complaints: Attorney General's Office.
  • Elections, corporations, charities, state archives and the State Library: Secretary of State.

Statewide elected offices

Washington voters elect nine statewide officials: the Governor, Lieutenant Governor, Secretary of State, Treasurer, Auditor, Attorney General, Commissioner of Public Lands, Superintendent of Public Instruction and Insurance Commissioner. Each runs an agency.

When you're stuck

  • Ask the agency's ombuds or constituent office, if it has one.
  • Contact your state legislators. Their offices help constituents with state agencies every day. Use Who represents you to find them.
