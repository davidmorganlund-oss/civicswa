State initiatives and referendums

Washington voters can write laws themselves and overturn laws the Legislature passes. Here is how each path works.

Three paths for voters

  • Initiative to the People. Sponsors file a proposed law, collect signatures, and if enough are valid, it goes straight to the November ballot. Signatures are due in early July.
  • Initiative to the Legislature. Signatures are due about 10 days before the legislative session starts in January. The Legislature can pass it as written. If it doesn't, the initiative goes to the next November ballot. The Legislature can also put its own alternative on the ballot next to it.
  • Referendum. Voters can ask to overturn a bill the Legislature just passed. Signatures are due within 90 days after the session ends. If enough are valid, the law is on hold until the vote.

How many signatures

The number is a share of all votes cast for Governor in the last governor's race: 8 percent for initiatives and 4 percent for referendums. Through 2028, that means:

  • Initiatives: 308,911 valid signatures.
  • Referendums: 154,456 valid signatures.

Campaigns usually collect more than the minimum, because some signatures turn out to be invalid.

Measures the Legislature sends you

  • Referendum bills. The Legislature can send a bill to voters instead of passing it into law.
  • Constitutional amendments. These need a two-thirds vote in both the House and Senate, then a majority of voters.

After an initiative passes

For two years after voters pass an initiative, the Legislature can change it only with a two-thirds vote in each chamber. After that, a simple majority can amend it.

How to weigh in

  • Read the voters' pamphlet, which includes each measure's text, an explanation, and arguments for and against.
  • Check who is funding each side through the Public Disclosure Commission.
  • Read before you sign a petition. You have the right to see the full text of a measure before signing.
