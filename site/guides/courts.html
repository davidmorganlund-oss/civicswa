How the courts work

Washington has two court systems side by side: state courts and federal courts. Most cases people deal with are in state court.

Washington's state courts

  • District and municipal courts handle misdemeanors, traffic tickets, small claims, and civil cases under a set dollar limit. District courts are run by counties, and municipal courts by cities.
  • Superior courts are the main trial courts, in every county. They hear felonies, larger civil cases, divorce and custody, juvenile cases, and appeals from district and municipal courts.
  • The Court of Appeals reviews most appeals from superior court. It has three divisions, based in Seattle, Tacoma and Spokane.
  • The Washington Supreme Court is the highest state court, with nine justices. It decides which cases it will review, and it has the final word on the Washington Constitution.

Washington voters elect their judges. When a seat opens mid-term, the Governor (or, for some local courts, the county or city) appoints someone to serve until the next election.

Federal courts

  • U.S. District Courts are federal trial courts. Washington has two: the Western District, based in Seattle and Tacoma, and the Eastern District, based in Spokane, Yakima and Richland.
  • The Ninth Circuit Court of Appeals hears appeals from federal courts in Washington and other western states.
  • The U.S. Supreme Court has the final word on federal law and the U.S. Constitution.

Federal judges are appointed by the President, confirmed by the Senate, and serve for life.

Which court handles what

  • State courts handle most crimes, family law, landlord-tenant disputes, and most contracts and injuries.
  • Federal courts handle federal crimes, cases involving federal law or the U.S. Constitution, and some disputes between people from different states.

How to take part

  • Watch. Most court hearings are open to the public, and the Washington Supreme Court's oral arguments are broadcast on TVW.
  • Serve on a jury when you're summoned. It's one of the most direct ways to take part in government.
  • Vote in judicial races, which appear on Washington ballots. Bar association ratings and the voters' pamphlet can help.
