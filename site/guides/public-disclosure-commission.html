The Public Disclosure Commission

The PDC shows where money in Washington politics comes from and where it goes. Anyone can use its data.

What it is

The Public Disclosure Commission was created in 1972 by a voter-approved initiative. Five commissioners appointed by the Governor and confirmed by the Senate lead it. It enforces the state's campaign finance and disclosure laws.

Who reports to it

  • Candidates and campaigns report contributions and spending.
  • Political committees, including those for and against ballot measures, report who funds them.
  • Lobbyists and their employers report what they spend to influence state government.
  • Many elected officials and candidates file a personal financial affairs statement, the F-1, listing income sources, investments and business interests.

What you can look up

  • Who gave to a candidate or measure, and how much.
  • How a campaign spent its money.
  • Who is lobbying, for whom, and what they reported spending.
  • An official's financial interests, to spot possible conflicts.

The PDC publishes this as open data on its website, searchable by name.

If you think something is wrong

Anyone can raise a concern about a possible violation with the PDC. Its website explains how complaints are reviewed. The Commission also meets in public, with time for public comment.
