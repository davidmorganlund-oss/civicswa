Local ballot measures

Levies, bonds and local propositions decide how much you pay for schools, fire service, parks and more. Here is how they work and what it takes to pass one.

What shows up on local ballots

  • Levies are property taxes for a set number of years, for things like school operations, fire and EMS, or parks.
  • Bonds let a government borrow money for construction, like new schools, and repay it with a property tax over many years.
  • Other propositions can change a city's form of government, create or merge districts, or set a local sales tax for a specific purpose.

Local measures can go on special election ballots in February and April as well as the August primary and November general elections.

What it takes to pass

  • School maintenance and operations levies pass with a simple majority: more than 50 percent yes.
  • School bonds need at least 60 percent yes, and enough people have to vote: turnout must be at least 40 percent of the number who voted in the last November general election.
  • Most other property tax levies beyond the regular limit also need 60 percent yes, plus a minimum number of yes votes based on the last general election's turnout.

This is why a school bond can get 58 percent yes and still fail.

Who can put a measure on the ballot

  • The governing body (a council, commission or school board) puts most measures on the ballot by resolution.
  • Voters can file local initiatives or referendums only where that power exists. Charter counties and cities that have adopted initiative and referendum powers allow it. Many others do not.

How to weigh in

  • Before it's on the ballot, comment when the board or council discusses the measure. That's when the amount, length and purpose are decided.
  • Read the voters' pamphlet. It includes the measure's text and statements for and against, often written by local residents. You can volunteer to write one through your county elections office.
  • Check your property tax statement after the election to see what each levy costs you.
