Volunteering and public service jobs

Some of the most direct ways to take part don't involve a vote at all. Here is how to find volunteer roles and public jobs.

Volunteer

  • Serve Washington, the state's service commission, runs AmeriCorps programs across the state and connects people to volunteer work.
  • Local governments use volunteers in parks, libraries, senior centers and emergency preparedness programs. Check your city or county website.
  • Elections offices hire temporary workers and need observers around each election. Contact your county elections office.
  • Boards and commissions are a form of volunteer service too. See serve on a board or commission.

Give

  • Check a charity before you give. The Secretary of State's office keeps a registry of charities that solicit in Washington, with their financial information.
  • Local giving goes far. Small community groups often do the most with the least.

Public service jobs

  • State jobs are posted on careers.wa.gov.
  • Federal jobs are posted on USAJOBS.
  • City, county, school district and port jobs are posted on each government's own website, and many use shared job boards.
  • Internships and fellowships are offered by the Legislature, state agencies and many local governments, often for students and recent graduates.

Public jobs range from engineers and nurses to planners, mechanics, analysts and clerks. Many offer student loan forgiveness eligibility through the federal Public Service Loan Forgiveness program.
