Serve on a board or commission

Hundreds of state, county and city boards shape rules, grants and services, and most are made up of ordinary residents. Here is how to get on one.

What boards and commissions do

Some boards make binding decisions, like licensing boards, college trustees and some state commissions. Others advise elected officials on topics like parks, human services or equity. Either way, members hear from the public, review proposals and vote on recommendations.

State boards

  • The Governor appoints most state board members. You can apply online through the Governor's Boards and Commissions office at any time, whether or not there is an opening.
  • Attach a resume and anything else you want considered to the online form.
  • Some seats require Senate confirmation, and some are set aside for people with specific experience, like a member of the public, a specific profession or a region of the state.
  • Applications are public records, so assume what you submit can be seen by others.

County and city boards

  • Cities and counties post openings on their websites, usually with a short application.
  • Councils or commissioners usually appoint members, sometimes after a brief interview at a public meeting.
  • Lived experience counts. Many boards look for members who use the services they oversee.

Before you apply

  • Attend a meeting or two to see the workload and the culture.
  • Ask about the time commitment. Some boards meet monthly, others weekly, plus reading.
  • Check for conflicts. Some appointments require financial disclosure or limit outside work related to the board.
