Federal elections and who can run

Washington sends 12 people to Congress and helps elect a President. Here is who can run for each office and how often.

The offices

  • U.S. House of Representatives. Washington has 10 House members, each elected from a congressional district for a two-year term. Every seat is on the ballot in every even-numbered year.
  • U.S. Senate. Washington has two senators, elected statewide for six-year terms. Their terms are staggered, so the seats come up in different years.
  • President and Vice President. Elected every four years. Washington's electoral votes go to the ticket that wins the state's popular vote.

Who can run (set by the U.S. Constitution)

  • House: at least 25 years old, a U.S. citizen for at least seven years, and a resident of the state. Members don't have to live in the district they represent.
  • Senate: at least 30 years old, a U.S. citizen for at least nine years, and a resident of the state.
  • President: at least 35 years old, a natural-born citizen, and a U.S. resident for at least 14 years. No one can be elected President more than twice.

How Washington runs these elections

  • House and Senate races use Washington's top-two primary in August. The top two candidates advance to November regardless of party.
  • Presidential nominations work differently. Washington holds a separate presidential primary in presidential election years, and the parties use the results to award delegates.
  • Candidates for Congress file during the state's May filing week and report campaign money to the Federal Election Commission, not the PDC.

How to follow your members

  • Find them with Who represents you.
  • Track their votes and bills on Congress.gov.
  • See who funds their campaigns at the Federal Election Commission's website, fec.gov.
